What is useful this week

Small Business Saturday as a measured operating test, the December 10 Working Lands deadline, 2026 information-return changes and a bottleneck question for year-end.

Best Practical Business Item

Make Small Business Saturday a measured operating test.

Small Business Saturday is November 28. Instead of treating it as a one-day discount event, choose one offer or customer action you want to learn from. Before the day, write down the baseline and the result you will track: average ticket, inquiries, repeat-customer visits, add-on sales, labor minutes or fulfillment backlog.

Why it matters: A promotion is more valuable when it teaches you what customers respond to and whether your operation can deliver the promise profitably.

Vermont / Working Lands

The Business Enhancement Grant deadline is December 10 at 2:00 PM.

The current Working Lands application window closes Thursday, December 10, at 2:00 PM. If the project fits, use the week after Thanksgiving to finish the scope, vendor quotes, financing plan and one measurable business result. If it does not fit, stop before application work crowds out the operating work.

Why it matters: A grant is useful only when the proposed investment solves a real constraint and the business can carry the project after the award period.

Year-End Records

Collect W-9s and reconcile contractor payments now.

The IRS guide lists a $2,000 reporting threshold for 2026 payments covered by Form 1099-NEC and several Form 1099-MISC categories. Businesses filing 10 or more information returns must e-file; the IRS says applying for a transmitter control code can take up to 45 days.

Why it matters: Confirm each payment category and payee before year-end, collect missing W-9s, and make sure the accounting file agrees with the amounts actually paid. Ask a tax professional about your specific facts.

Official IRS guide →

Operations Checkpoint

Find the holiday bottleneck before demand finds it for you.

Draw the path from order to cash: inquiry, quote, scheduling, production, delivery, invoicing and collection. Circle the step with the longest queue or the most rework. Protect that step first; speeding up everything else may only make the queue bigger.

Worth Discussing at the Next Meeting

If demand increased 20 percent next week, what would fail first?

Name the constraint, the early warning signal and one small protective action you can take before December.
