Best Practical Business Item

Close the quarter before the extended Form 941 deadline.

The IRS tax calendar lists November 10 as the third-quarter Form 941 deadline for employers that timely deposited and paid all required employment taxes. Agricultural employers may instead use Form 943, and businesses assigned to annual Form 944 should not file Form 941.

Why it matters: A filing deadline is also a useful control point. Reconcile payroll reports to the general ledger, confirm deposits, resolve employee classifications and keep the filed return with its supporting reports. The objective is not merely to submit a form; it is to finish the quarter with numbers that agree.

Vermont / Working Lands

Use the November 12 Q&A to test one specific project.

Vermont's Business Enhancement Grant remains open through December 10, 2026, at 2:00 PM. Awards are $10,000–$50,000, no match is required, and the program recommends applying for the minimum amount needed for one successful project. The next virtual Q&A is Thursday, November 12, 3:30–4:30 PM. A few hours of project-planning assistance through the Farm & Forest Viability Program are also available first-come, first-served.

Why it matters: Bring a one-sentence bottleneck, the proposed solution, a rough budget and one measurable result. Use the session to test eligibility and scope before polishing the application.

Financing / Timing

The Business & Industry portal has a November 20 cutoff.

USDA Rural Development says its OneRD platform will stop accepting new Business & Industry Guaranteed Loan applications and servicing requests on Friday, November 20, at 5:00 PM Eastern. A replacement portal is expected to open in January 2027. B&I applications are submitted by lenders, not directly by businesses.

Why it matters: If a lender is preparing a B&I-backed request, ask which side of the transition it belongs on and whether the current file can be complete before the cutoff. Do not rush a weak application merely to beat a system change; clarify the plan, missing documents and likely January path with the lender and state office.

Field Operations / Winter

Write the cold-weather stop rules before the weather makes the decision.

OSHA's winter-weather guidance tells employers to train workers to recognize cold stress, monitor one another, use warm areas and breaks, maintain reliable communications and be able to stop work or evacuate. It also recommends scheduling cold-exposed work for warmer parts of the day when possible.

Why it matters: A useful one-page plan names the forecast threshold or site condition that changes the day, who checks in with remote workers, where dry replacement clothing and warm-up space are located, and who has authority to stop the job.
