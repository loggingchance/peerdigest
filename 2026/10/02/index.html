Best Practical Business Item

Do not let a grant become a shopping list

Vermont’s FY27 Working Lands Business Enhancement Grant opens October 9. Awards are expected to range from $10,000 to $50,000, there is no match requirement, and eligible project categories include equipment, infrastructure, market development, research and development, and workforce training. The state also says applicants should focus on one project and expect to submit a profit-and-loss statement, balance sheet, and cash-flow statement.

Why it matters: The useful question is not “what could I buy with $50,000?” It is “what single constraint is holding back throughput, margin, or capacity, and what investment would measurably remove it?” A grant that increases fixed cost without relieving the real bottleneck can make the business worse after the grant ends.

Useful for: Vermont farms, logging and forest-product businesses, firewood, sawmills, and eligible value-added operations; also useful as a planning discipline for everyone.

Pricing / Cash Flow / Risk

Disaster reimbursement starts with records, not the application

Vermont’s Timber Disaster Assistance Program is expected to open in October for qualifying 2023 and 2024 weather losses. Eligible categories include standing and harvested timber losses, certain costs to resume logging and restore AMP compliance, forest-management-plan updates, and licensed-forester assessments. The program says 95% of eligible forest-plan update costs can be covered, and logger claims can include truck-road and landing hardening, skid-trail improvement, and temporary stream crossings.

Why it matters: Reimbursement programs reward businesses that can reconstruct what happened. Photos, contracts, receipts, dates, invoices, and job notes are not clerical overhead when they are the evidence needed to recover money after a disruption.

Useful for: Jeff and other forestry-service/logging businesses, foresters, forest landowners, and anyone whose work is vulnerable to weather-driven access loss.

Grants / Programs / Opportunities

SARE is large enough to test a business model, not merely teach one

Northeast SARE’s 2027 Farming Community Grant proposals are due October 26 at 4 p.m. ET. The program has about $1.5 million available, with typical requests in the $250,000–$500,000 range, and can support projects involving marketing and business, livestock, economic and social sustainability, and other farmer-led innovations.

Why it matters: This is too large and collaborative for most single owner-operators to chase casually. The opportunity is a partnership around a repeatable problem: targeted-grazing business models, workforce systems, market development, recurring service structures, or another experiment that can be tested across several farms and then replicated.

Useful for: Adam and other farm/service businesses as partners; FBS-type training/research collaborations; businesses with a problem that is shared by a wider farming community.

Labor / Subcontractors / Benefits

A new retirement match changes the conversation for some small-business workers

IRS Notice 2026-48 says that for tax years beginning after December 31, 2026, eligible lower- and moderate-income savers can receive a federal Saver’s Match of up to 50% of the first $2,000 they contribute to a qualifying retirement account, for a maximum match of $1,000. The IRS specifically notes the program’s relevance to independent contractors, self-employed workers, and people without employer-sponsored retirement plans.

Why it matters: Small working-lands businesses often cannot compete with large employers on benefit packages. A simple retirement-savings conversation, coupled with a low-cost IRA or an existing small-employer plan, may be more valuable to some workers than another small wage increase — especially when federal matching money is available.

Useful for: sole proprietors, owner-operators, seasonal employers, businesses with lower- and moderate-wage employees, and anyone reviewing retirement savings before 2027.

Worth Discussing at the Next Meeting

If someone handed you $40,000 tomorrow, what exact constraint would you remove first — and what number would prove that it worked?

A good answer is not “buy a machine,” “hire someone,” or “do more marketing.” Name the constraint, the intervention, and the measure: productive hours, jobs completed, margin per constrained hour, backlog reduced, callbacks avoided, repeat revenue added, or owner time released.
