Best Practical Business Item

Working Lands funding is live. Build the application around one measurable constraint.

Vermont’s Business Enhancement Grant is open through December 10, 2026, at 2 p.m. Awards range from $10,000 to $50,000, and the official request for applications says no matching funds are required. Eligible uses include projects that improve working-lands business operations, markets, infrastructure, equipment, workforce or product development.

Why it matters: A list of purchases is not a business case. Name the bottleneck, show its current cost in time, margin or lost sales, and state the measurable result the project should produce. Include installation, training, permitting and ongoing costs in the decision.

Useful for: qualifying Vermont farm, forest-products and food businesses. Apply for the minimum amount that fully solves the defined problem, and leave time for WebGrants access and required quotes.

Energy / Capital Planning

REAP grants are not open. The guaranteed-loan path is still available.

USDA Rural Development currently says it is not accepting Rural Energy for America Program grant applications, while guaranteed-loan applications may be submitted. An October 2 final rule also says a future national grant competition will require projects to be built, operating and able to document performance before applying; USDA has not yet announced the next grant window.

Why it matters: Do not buy equipment on the assumption that a grant is available. If an energy project makes sense without a grant, preserve baseline utility or fuel records, quotes, commissioning records and measured performance. If financing is needed now, ask a lender and the state energy coordinator about the guaranteed-loan path.

Useful for: agricultural producers and eligible rural small businesses considering energy-efficiency or renewable-energy investments. The grant and guaranteed-loan routes are different decisions with different timing.

Financing / Intermediaries

A zero-interest rural-business loan starts with the utility, not the business.

The Rural Economic Development Loan and Grant program lets eligible electric or telecommunications utilities borrow USDA funds and pass zero-interest loans to rural businesses for projects that create or retain jobs. The current program page says regular competitions are waiting for a full-year appropriation and a cleared federal funding notice, so this is a preparation path—not a promise of immediate money.

Why it matters: A small business is the ultimate recipient, not the USDA applicant. If expansion, equipment or working capital would create or retain jobs, identify the local eligible utility and prepare a concise project scope, job impact, sources and uses, repayment plan and timing. The utility decides whether to sponsor a project.

Useful for: rural businesses with a defined project and a utility willing to serve as intermediary. First-time loans can be zero-interest, may cover up to 80 percent of project cost, and require the balance from the business or utility.

Markets / State Programs

Specialty-crop money is moving through states. Watch for the subaward, not a federal form.

USDA announced $86.6 million for about 609 Specialty Crop Block Grant projects across 56 states and territories. The money supports state-administered projects for fruits, vegetables, tree nuts, nursery crops and other specialty crops, including marketing, food safety, research and pest or disease work.

Why it matters: This announcement is not a direct federal grant round for an individual farm. Businesses should track the Vermont Agency of Agriculture’s specialty-crop program and industry partners for state subawards, project participation or market-development work that matches a real sales barrier.

Useful for: farms, nurseries, maple and specialty-food businesses, and service providers that can contribute to a multi-business market, research or supply-chain project.

Worth Discussing at the Next Meeting

Which opportunity survives when the subsidy is removed?

Take one equipment, energy or expansion project on your list. Does it still improve cash flow at ordinary financing terms? What evidence would prove the bottleneck, and what number would tell you the project worked six months after completion?

Bring the smallest version of the project that produces a measurable result. That is usually the best starting point for a grant, loan or self-funded decision.
