Best Practical Business Item

Federal dyed-diesel penalties are suspended. Keep the relief in its lane.

The IRS announced October 14 that it will not impose federal excise-tax penalties when dyed diesel is sold for highway use or used in diesel-powered highway vehicles. The relief is nationwide, retroactive to October 1, and runs through December 31, 2026. The IRS also says the relief is federal only and does not change state fuel taxes or state penalties.

Why it matters: This can affect logging, trucking, farming, and service businesses that hold dyed fuel in bulk tanks. Our practical recommendation is to record the date, gallons, vehicle, job, and supplier anyway, even though the IRS says federal substantiation records are not required during the relief period. Those records help separate federal relief from state obligations and preserve job-cost information.

Useful for: businesses considering highway use of dyed diesel during the temporary relief period. Check current Vermont guidance before changing a fuel practice, and do not treat a federal penalty suspension as a blanket tax exemption.

Grants / Capital Decisions

Working Lands funding is open. Start with the constraint, not the shopping list.

Vermont’s Working Lands Business Enhancement Grant opened October 9 and closes December 10 at 2 p.m. Awards range from $10,000 to $50,000, with up to $508,000 available and no match requirement. Eligible project categories include marketing, infrastructure or construction, equipment, workforce development, and research and development for qualifying farm, forest-products, and Vermont-ingredient food businesses.

Why it matters: A competitive application should connect an investment to a measurable business result. Name the current bottleneck, document its cost, estimate the improvement, and include ongoing expenses. Compare the project with a lower-cost process change before asking grant funds to solve the problem.

Useful for: qualifying Vermont primary and secondary forest-products businesses, farms, and food businesses. The program notes that applicants may use limited project-planning support and should apply for the minimum amount needed.

Services / Associations

A November 5 grant can fund the support system around working-lands businesses.

The Working Lands Service Provider & Producer Association Grant closes November 5 at 2 p.m. Up to $225,000 is available, awards start at $10,000, no match is required, and the program says most recent awards were under $40,000. Eligible applicants are organizations providing business support or Vermont producer associations—not individual businesses applying for their own equipment.

Why it matters: Peer groups, trade associations, and technical-assistance organizations can propose market development, business planning, disaster preparedness, succession, access to capital, manufacturing efficiency, workforce development, or association-building work. A strong concept should identify a defined cohort, a repeated business problem, the service delivered, and a result that can be measured.

Useful for: organizations and associations serving Vermont farm, forest, food, beverage, or fiber businesses. WebGrants registration may take up to two business days, so an eligible applicant should not wait until deadline day.

Data / Operations

Use the FIA symposium to sharpen one operating question.

The USDA Forest Service’s virtual Forest Inventory and Analysis Science Symposium runs October 19–22. FIA says the event is designed for technical exchange among people working with inventory and monitoring data, and its program tools include forest inventory, timber-products output, ownership, and geospatial resources.

Why it matters: A small business does not need to become a research shop. Pick one question before attending: local timber supply, product flow, ownership trends, or how a measurement method might improve an estimate or proposal. Capture one data source, one decision it could improve, and one limit that keeps the data from being overinterpreted.

Useful for: forestry consultants, inventory contractors, forest-products businesses, and organizations that need credible forest-resource data.

Worth Discussing at the Next Meeting

Which temporary rule change belongs in a permanent operating record?

Choose one current relief measure, grant opportunity, or data source. What decision could it change this month? What record would let you explain that decision to your accountant, lender, employee, or future self?

Bring one example. The goal is not more paperwork; it is a short trail from source fact to business decision to measured result.
