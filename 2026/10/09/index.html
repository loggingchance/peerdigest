Best Practical Business Item

The application is open. Write the investment case before the equipment list.

Vermont’s Working Lands Business Enhancement Grant opened October 9. The application deadline is December 10 at 2 p.m.; an applicant webinar is October 14, 11 a.m.–noon. The overview lists $10,000–$50,000 awards with no match requirement. The program page offers a referral for a few hours of project-planning help through the Farm & Forest Viability Program.

Why it matters: Our practical test is a one-page investment case: the constraint, proposed expense, current performance, expected improvement, and ongoing cost. For a logging contractor that might mean truck turnaround or repairs; for a farm or service business, scheduling or repeat orders. Compare the investment with the option of fixing the process first.

Useful for: eligible Vermont farm, forest, and value-added businesses preparing a focused project. Check the current RFA for eligibility and expense rules before committing money.

Value-Added / Recurring Revenue

Predictable demand can help both sides of a supply relationship.

A Working Lands case study describes Butterfly Bakery of Vermont’s grant-supported marketing work and its advance contracts with farm suppliers. The company sources produce from 17 Vermont farms. The report says combined hot sauce and caramel sales grew 24% in June–November 2025 compared with the same period in 2024.

Why it matters: This is an illustration, not proof that a marketing campaign alone caused the growth. Our takeaway is to connect a clearer sales plan with more predictable purchasing. For firewood, grazing, fencing, or maintenance services, a useful experiment could be asking existing customers about next season before adding capacity. Record booked work, gross margin, and delivery cost; a bigger sales month alone does not tell you whether the business improved.

Useful for: businesses with repeat buyers and suppliers who need advance notice. Agree on scope, timing, and pricing before treating interest as committed revenue.

Pricing / Cash Flow / Risk

Use the lead time to assemble a loss file.

Vermont has announced October 26 as the opening date for its Agriculture and Timber Disaster Assistance Program, with applications running into late December. It covers qualifying 2023–2024 weather losses for eligible agricultural and timber producers, forest landowners, and contracted loggers. It does not include 2025 drought impacts or pay again for losses covered by another program.

Why it matters: Our suggestion is to build one dated loss file now: photos, affected job or property, contracts, repair invoices, receipts, and prior assistance. Separate the original loss from later improvements. Missing records are easier to recover before a deadline becomes urgent. Keep any hoped-for payment separate from cash you already have when planning winter expenses.

Useful for: Vermont operations with potentially qualifying losses. Category-specific rules and documentation requirements determine eligibility; confirm those with the program before counting on reimbursement.

Grants / Programs / Opportunities

A partnership must fit the grant’s purpose.

Northeast SARE’s 2027 Farming Community proposals are due October 26 at 4 p.m. Eastern. The program prioritizes regional and multistate work at the intersection of nutrition and health, people, animals, and pathogens. Marketing and business projects can fit within that scope, but a general business workshop does not automatically qualify.

Why it matters: Last week’s broad partnership idea needs this qualification. Our first step would be a short scope conversation with SARE before assembling a large proposal. Name the farmer problem, program priority, partners, and result another business could reproduce. General public education and awareness campaigns are outside this program’s scope.

Useful for: farmer-led partnerships, agricultural service providers, and research or education collaborators with a strong fit. A separate Vermont Farm to Institution Market Development grant targets sustained sales to institutions; its deadline is October 26 at 1:59 p.m. and it requires a 25% match. Read its specific eligibility rules if that is your actual project.

Worth Discussing at the Next Meeting

Which customer commitment would make next season easier to plan?

Choose one repeat buyer and one recurring job. What scope, timing, quantity, or deposit would turn a hopeful forecast into usable information? Then name the measure that would show an improvement: booked work, margin per crew-day, fewer empty trips, or less owner time chasing the next sale.

Bring one example from your business. If a grant could help, describe the business decision first and the funding route second.
