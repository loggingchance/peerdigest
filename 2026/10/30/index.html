Best Practical Business Item

Build the tax packet before year-end.

The IRS says year-round tax planning makes filing easier and reminds self-employed people that estimated payments may be required. Before December gets crowded, reconcile bank and card accounts, match major purchases to receipts, update mileage and owner-draw records, and compare estimated payments with the year's actual profit trend.

Why it matters: January is a poor time to discover a missing W-9, an undocumented equipment purchase, or a quarter of unreconciled transactions. A clean packet also gives your adviser time to discuss timing decisions before the year closes.

Useful for: any owner who wants a faster close, fewer surprises, and better numbers for pricing and borrowing.

Labor / Contractor Risk

A 1099 and a contract do not make someone a contractor.

The U.S. Department of Labor's current small-entity guide says worker status under the Fair Labor Standards Act depends on economic reality. The six factors include opportunity for profit or loss, investments, permanence, control, whether the work is integral to the business, and skill and initiative. No single factor decides the question, and a written label is not enough.

Why it matters: Review recurring subcontractor relationships before year-end forms are issued. Write down who sets price and schedule, who supplies major equipment, whether the worker markets to other customers, and how central the work is to your usual business. If the facts are mixed, get qualified advice before repeating the arrangement.

Vermont / Working Lands

Use the November 4 Q&A to test the project before polishing the narrative.

Vermont's Business Enhancement Grant is open through December 10, 2026, at 2:00 PM. Awards are $10,000–$50,000, no match is required, and eligible projects can address equipment, infrastructure, markets, research and development, or workforce needs. The next virtual question-and-answer session is Wednesday, November 4, 10:00–11:00 AM.

Why it matters: Bring a one-sentence bottleneck, a rough sources-and-uses budget, and the result you will measure. Ask whether the proposed cost and business history fit before spending hours on prose.

Financing

The Business & Industry guarantee starts with a lender.

USDA Rural Development describes Business & Industry guarantees as a way for approved commercial lenders to finance eligible rural businesses. The lender—not the business—is the federal applicant. A useful first packet includes the project scope, sources and uses, ownership, historical financials, projections, collateral, and a plain explanation of repayment.

Why it matters: A federal guarantee is not a substitute for cash flow. It can improve a lender's risk position, but the business still needs a credible project and repayment case. Start with the lender and USDA Rural Development state office before assuming a project fits.
